Simple Habits

Daily Online Safety Tips

Follow these everyday guidelines to protect your passwords, payment apps, and personal details from online scammers.

🔑

1. Password & OTP Rules

  • Never share OTPs: No bank employee, police officer, or company agent will ever ask for your OTP.
  • Use long passwords: Combine words, numbers, and symbols (like BlueSky#River99) rather than simple names or birth dates.
  • Use different passwords: Do not use the same password for banking, email, and social media.
  • Keep screen codes hidden: Turn off lock-screen message previews so strangers cannot read your OTP without unlocking your phone.
💳

2. UPI & Payment Safety

  • UPI PIN is only to SEND money: You never need to enter your PIN or scan a QR code to receive money.
  • Check the name before paying: Look at the screen to verify the person or shop name before entering your PIN.
  • Never scan QR codes to receive refunds: Scanning any QR code will only deduct money from your account.
  • Set daily transfer limits: Use your banking app to set a sensible daily limit on UPI payments.
🌐

3. Safe Browsing Habits

  • Check website spelling: Look carefully at website names. Scammers use fake links like sbi-portal.xyz.
  • Never install apps from chat messages: Only download apps from the official Google Play Store or Apple App Store.
  • Bookmark official websites: Save your official banking websites in your bookmarks so you don't click fake search ads.
  • Do not trust fake customer care numbers on Google: Always take phone numbers from the back of your card or official bill.
🛡️

4. Personal Information Safety

  • Use Masked Aadhaar: When sharing ID with hotels or vendors, download Masked Aadhaar showing only the last 4 digits.
  • Never install screen-sharing apps for strangers: Never download AnyDesk, TeamViewer, or QuickSupport if someone asks over the phone.
  • Avoid public Wi-Fi for payments: Do not log into your bank account on open, free public Wi-Fi networks.
  • Report a dead SIM card immediately: If your phone suddenly loses cellular network for no reason, contact your mobile operator right away.
Quick Checklist

The Essential Do's & Don'ts

Keep this simple mental checklist in mind every time you use the internet.

✓ DO'S (Good Habits)

  • ✓ Always check who sent an SMS or email before replying.
  • ✓ Call the number on the back of your bank card if you have any doubts.
  • ✓ Check your bank account statements regularly every month.
  • ✓ Choose Cash on Delivery when shopping on new or unfamiliar websites.
  • ✓ Call helpline 1930 immediately if money is taken without your knowledge.

✗ DON'TS (Dangerous Mistakes)

  • ✗ Do NOT enter your UPI PIN to receive money, cashback, or prizes.
  • ✗ Do NOT share OTPs, passwords, or card CVV numbers with anyone.
  • ✗ Do NOT download AnyDesk, TeamViewer, or unknown APK files for strangers.
  • ✗ Do NOT panic when receiving threats that your power or bank will be cut off.
  • ✗ Do NOT pay upfront "registration" or "deposit" fees for online jobs.

Emergency Help

What to Do If You Get Scammed

If you or someone in your family loses money to a cyber scam, acting quickly can help freeze and recover the money.

National Helpline

Dial 1930 (Cyber Fraud Helpline)

Helpline 1930 is a 24/7 toll-free service operated by the Government of India. When you call, police and banks work together to freeze the stolen money before the scammer can withdraw it from an ATM.

Visit cybercrime.gov.in ↗

3 Simple Steps If You Are Scammed:

1

Step 1: Call Your Bank Immediately to Block the Card/UPI

Call the customer care number on the back of your debit card to block your card, UPI, and netbanking so no more money can be taken.

2

Step 2: Dial 1930 Right Away (The Faster, The Better)

Give the police officer your name, phone number, bank name, and the transaction details (UTR number from the SMS). They will issue an alert to freeze the scammer's account.

3

Step 3: Register a Complaint on cybercrime.gov.in

Visit the official website cybercrime.gov.in and submit a formal complaint with screenshots of the messages, the scammer's phone number, and your bank statement.
