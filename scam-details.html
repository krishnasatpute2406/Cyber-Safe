Detailed Guide

How Common Online Scams Work

Each scam below is explained in four simple parts: What is it, how it happens, the warning signs, and how to stay safe.

Scam 01 · Fake Messages & Websites · High Risk

1. Phishing Scams

What is it?

Phishing is a scam where criminals pretend to be a trustworthy organization—like your bank, the electricity board, or a delivery company. They send you a fake text message, email, or WhatsApp message with a link. When you click the link, it opens a fake website that looks almost identical to the real one, designed to steal your passwords and card details.

How does it happen?

1. The Fake Alert: You receive a message saying: "Your bank account will be blocked today due to pending PAN card update. Click this link immediately."
2. The Fake Website: You click the link, which opens a website with your bank's logo and colors, but the website address is slightly different (for example, sbi-update-online.xyz instead of the real bank address).
3. Entering Your Secrets: The page asks you to type in your username, password, card number, and OTP.
4. Money Stolen: The scammer immediately uses your details to log into your real account and transfer money away.

Warning signs

• Urgent threats: Messages that panic you with deadlines like "Account blocked within 24 hours" or "Power cut tonight at 9 PM".
• Strange website addresses: Look closely at the website link. Legitimate banks use official dot-com or official bank domains, not random addresses ending in .xyz, .top, or shortened links like bit.ly.
• Sent from personal phone numbers: Official banks and utility companies send SMS using letters (like VK-SBIINB), never a regular 10-digit mobile number.
• Asking for secret details: Any website asking for your card PIN, CVV, or passwords via a text message link is fake.

How to stay safe

• Never click links in SMS or WhatsApp: If you receive a message about your bank or electricity bill, never tap the link inside the message.
• Type the website address yourself: Always open your browser and type the official website address directly, or use the official mobile app downloaded from the app store.
• Call the official customer care: If you are unsure, look up the customer care number on the back of your bank debit card or on your physical bill and call them directly.

Scam 02 · Secret Code Theft · High Risk

2. OTP Fraud

What is it?

OTP stands for One-Time Password. It is the secret 4-digit or 6-digit code sent to your mobile phone to verify an online payment or login. OTP fraud happens when a scammer convinces you to read out your OTP over a phone call, or tricks you into installing an app that lets them see your phone screen.

How does it happen?

1. The Panic Call: You get a call from someone claiming to be a bank manager or credit card officer: "Sir, someone is trying to take ₹25,000 from your account right now! I need to block it immediately."
2. Asking for the Code: While talking to you, the scammer is actually on a shopping website trying to spend your money. Your bank sends an OTP to your phone to confirm the payment.
3. The Lie: The caller says: "I just sent you a cancellation code. Please read it to me so I can stop the payment."
4. Money Deducted: As soon as you tell them the code, the payment goes through and the money is deducted from your account.

Warning signs

• The caller asks for your OTP: No real bank employee, police officer, or government official will ever ask you for your OTP.
• Creating extreme panic: The caller tells you that you will lose money or your card will be canceled if you don't act within two minutes.
• Asking you to install screen apps: The caller instructs you to download apps like AnyDesk, TeamViewer QuickSupport, or RustDesk onto your smartphone. These apps allow them to see everything on your screen.
• Reading the SMS carefully: The SMS with the code clearly says: "OTP for transaction of ₹..." and "Do not share with anyone".

How to stay safe

• Never share your OTP with anyone: Treat your OTP like your house key. Never read it out to anyone over the phone, no matter who they claim to be.
• Always read the full SMS: Before using any OTP, read the message. Check how much money is being spent and which company is receiving it.
• Never install screen-sharing apps on caller instructions: Never download AnyDesk or TeamViewer because a stranger on the phone told you to.
• Cut the call: If someone demands your OTP, hang up immediately and call your bank's official number to check your balance.

Scam 03 · Payment & QR Code Traps · High Risk

3. UPI Scams

What is it?

UPI (Unified Payments Interface) is used on apps like Google Pay, PhonePe, and Paytm. UPI scams happen when scammers exploit the confusion between sending money and receiving money. They trick you into entering your UPI PIN by claiming it is required to receive payment, cashback, or an item refund.

How does it happen?

1. The Fake Buyer: You list an old sofa, phone, or bike for sale on a website like OLX. A buyer contacts you saying they want to buy it immediately without bargaining.
2. The Fake QR Code: The buyer sends you a QR code on WhatsApp and says: "Scan this QR code in Google Pay and enter your UPI PIN to claim the ₹10,000 payment."
3. The Mistake: You scan the QR code and type your 4-digit or 6-digit UPI PIN.
4. Money Debited: Instead of receiving ₹10,000, ₹10,000 is immediately deducted from your bank account and sent to the scammer.

Warning signs

• Being told to enter a PIN to receive money: This is always a scam. Entering a UPI PIN always deducts money from your account.
• Being sent a QR code to receive payment: Scanning a QR code is only for paying, never for receiving.
• Receiving a "Collect Request" notification: An alert popping up on your UPI app asking you to approve a payment or pay money.
• Fake identity claims: Scammers often pretend to be army officers, police personnel, or government workers to gain quick trust.

How to stay safe

• Remember the Golden Rule of UPI: You NEVER need to enter your UPI PIN to receive money. A PIN is only used when YOU are paying.
• To receive money, just give your phone number or UPI ID: The sender only needs your mobile number or UPI ID to send you money directly. No scanning or PIN is needed from you.
• Decline payment requests from strangers: If you receive an unexpected request to pay money inside your payment app, tap "Decline" immediately.
• Check your bank balance directly: Never trust a screenshot sent on WhatsApp showing money transferred. Open your banking app and verify your actual balance.

Scam 04 · Work From Home Traps · Medium Risk

4. Fake Job Scams

What is it?

Fake job scams target students, homemakers, and job seekers looking for part-time income. Scammers send messages offering huge salaries for very simple online work—such as liking YouTube videos, rating hotels on Google Maps, or doing simple typing tasks. In the end, they ask you to deposit your own money to unlock your "earnings".

How does it happen?

1. The WhatsApp Invitation: You get a message saying: "Work from home for 1 hour daily and earn ₹3,000–₹5,000 liking YouTube videos."
2. The Bait Payout: You complete 3 test tasks (like subscribing to a channel), and they actually send ₹150 or ₹200 to your bank account to make you believe it is real.
3. The Telegram Group Trap: They invite you to a Telegram group and introduce "Prepaid Tasks": "Deposit ₹3,000 to earn ₹4,500 profit in 30 minutes."
4. Trapped Money: After you send money, their fake website shows you have a huge balance, but tells you: "To withdraw your money, you must pay a ₹10,000 tax/verification fee." You can never withdraw your money.

Warning signs

• Unrealistic pay for simple tasks: Earning thousands of rupees per day just for clicking "Like" or typing numbers is too good to be true.
• Job offers sent out of the blue on WhatsApp or Telegram: Real companies do not recruit candidates through random WhatsApp texts.
• Asking you to pay money to get your salary: Legitimate employers pay you; they never ask you to pay a registration or security fee.
• Anonymous Telegram admins: All communication happens inside anonymous chat groups where everyone else posting screenshots of profits are fake accounts or bots.

How to stay safe

• Never pay money to get a job: A real job will never ask you for an upfront deposit, training fee, or "VIP task" fee.
• Apply through verified company portals: Look for job openings on official company career pages or trusted platforms like LinkedIn.
• Block unsolicited recruitment messages: If an unknown number messages you offering easy daily money, block and report the number immediately.
• Do not get tricked by small initial payments: Scammers willingly give ₹200 at the start to steal ₹50,000 later. Take the lesson and stop immediately.

Scam 05 · Fake Online Stores · Medium Risk

5. Online Shopping Scams

What is it?

Online shopping fraud happens through fake e-commerce websites and advertisements on Instagram, Facebook, and Google. These websites display branded shoes, watches, clothes, or electronics at ridiculously low prices (like 80% to 90% off). Once you pay online, they either send nothing or deliver cheap, broken junk.

How does it happen?

1. The Eye-Catching Ad: While scrolling social media, you see an advertisement: "Clearance Sale! Brand new Sony wireless headphones for ₹899 (Regular price ₹15,000)."
2. The Fake Store: Clicking the ad opens a professional-looking website with customer reviews and countdown timers saying: "Sale ends in 15 minutes!"
3. Payment Trap: When you try to place an order, the website says "Cash on Delivery not available in your area" and forces you to pay in advance with UPI or card.
4. Vanishing Act: After paying, you receive no tracking information, customer service numbers do not work, and the website disappears within a few weeks.

Warning signs

• Prices that are too low to be real: A brand new iPhone or Nike shoe for ₹999 is 100% a scam.
• Fake countdown urgency timers: Clocks on the screen urging you to buy before the time runs out.
• Cash on Delivery (COD) disabled: They refuse to let you pay when the product arrives at your door.
• No real contact information: The website has no physical office address, no registered company name, and only a fake email address for support.

How to stay safe

• If a deal looks too good to be true, it is: Quality products have real manufacturing costs; nobody sells ₹15,000 electronics for ₹999.
• Use Cash on Delivery for new websites: When ordering from an unfamiliar website for the first time, always choose Cash on Delivery so you only pay once the package is in your hand.
• Stick to well-known shopping apps: Buy from established, verified platforms that have clear return and refund guarantees.
• Search for reviews online: Before buying, search the website name on Google along with the word "review" or "scam" to see if others were cheated.
